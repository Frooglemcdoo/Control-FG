# R449: image stability before performance

User result: R448 worsened FPS and did not fix speckles. Reissuing the same ZIP
was a delivery correction, not a rendering fix. Do not request another R448 run.
Performance optimization is deferred; the eventual goal remains 1-2 FPS cost.

## Estimator defect in scope

R448 uses a cosine pilot C0 and nine mixture samples. Its result is
A*C0 + sum(w*C)/10, where A=1-sum(w)/10. It is unbiased under the documented
assumptions, but A is random. A rare bright pilot hit can receive much more than
one tenth of its value. Its bound is positivity, not a useful outlier bound.
For a rare region with cosine probability .001 and a perfect guide, an exact
finite enumeration gives R448 variance .00002087108 versus native .0000999
for unit source brightness: a reduction, but still a substantial tail. This
model is an optimistic oracle, not evidence of guide quality in the game.
Existing uploaded compact logs predate a demonstrated GI_AUTO activation and
cannot establish this as the observed R448 failure's sole cause.

## Change

All ten fresh directions now use the mixture density, stratified by selector.
For valid targets q=.6*p+.2*g0+.2*g1: two samples per cone and six cosine samples.
Every complete RGB contribution uses p/q then divides by ten. There is no pilot
residual coefficient, clamp, blur, reused radiance, or additional ray. Original
independent range hashes and native GI hit-distance convention are retained.
The same frozen targets and sampler are used by the producer and evaluator.
The balance-heuristic estimator follows Veach chapter 9:
https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs348b-03/papers/veach-chapter9.pdf

Empty/invalid guides use the previous ten-ray sampling. The original prototype
GTSample function retains its .1 cap for existing callers. The auto route allows
.4 and its binding contract rejects larger/nonfinite fractions. Training samples
also use the corrected all-ray weights.

## Validation and limits

GPU fixture tests mean constant-field energy (not per-pixel exact constancy),
cosine integrals, front/behind/empty/stale/one-bound/nonfinite states, original
range draws and distinct directions. Additional narrow-source fixtures check
energy, variance and peak for aligned and moved sources, preserve support with
stale bounds, and require exactly zero with a blocked source. These are shader
and analytic tests, not a substitute for Control's native traversal/lighting.

The two-global-cell learner remains limited: irrelevant or stale bounds can
increase variance. There is no claim of a complete speckle fix or FPS recovery.
The live-path logs identify GI_AUTO_R449 and estimator=stratified_balance; both
producer and evaluator must appear before assessing its image-quality result.

The first R449 GPU pass preserved mean energy and bounded the aligned narrow
source peak near 1.25 for a mean of 1, but showed substantial variance on a smooth
z-squared field. Follow-up adds independently rotated radial strata within each
component (six cosine strata and two per cone). This keeps the full marginal per
angular stratum while preserving coverage of smooth indirect illumination.
